# Local exchanges characterize every grouped-minor repair

**The linear repair problem has a complete local description.** For any finite
multiplicity pattern, every change of signed coloring weights that preserves
all grouped squared-minor statistics is a linear combination of alternating
six-term moves on **three coordinates and three distinct colors**.

For the first missing three-color pattern $(2,2,2)$, there are 90 colorings,
120 such moves, and a 47-dimensional repair space. An explicit 47-move basis
reconstructs all 90 coefficients of the earlier
[positive neighborhood correction](../three-color-coupling/README.md).

This is a theorem about linear freedom, **not universal positivity**. We give
the exact remaining cone-dual condition below. We also prove that averaging
canonical masses over unitary changes of basis inside the color eigenspaces
cannot help: every individual canonical mass is invariant. A known four-color
counterexample calibrates why complete local generation alone cannot imply
nonnegative certificates.

## 1. The statistic map and elementary moves

Fix nonnegative integers $r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1}$ with sum $n$, and let
$\mathcal K_r$ be the colorings of $[n]$ with these multiplicities. Define the
real linear map $A_r$ on signed weights by

$$(A_rh)_{S,\nu}=\sum_{\kappa:\,|S\cap\kappa^{-1}(j)|=\nu_j\ \forall j}h_\kappa.$$

All subsets $S\subseteq[n]$ are included, including the empty set, so total
mass is one of the statistics. A repair direction is an element of $\ker A_r$.

Choose three coordinates $i_0<i_1<i_2$, three colors $j_0<j_1<j_2$ with positive
multiplicity, and a coloring of the remaining coordinates with residual counts
$r_j-\mathbf1_{j\in\{j_0,j_1,j_2\}}$. On the six completions obtained by
permuting those three colors among the selected coordinates, assign coefficient
$\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)$; assign zero elsewhere. Call this vector a
**three-site exchange**. It has three $+1$ and three $-1$ entries.

Every such exchange lies in $\ker A_r$. If a subset meets the three sites in
zero or three places, the six signs sum to zero. If it meets them in one or two
places, each fixed count outcome has two completions with opposite signs.
The outside coloring contributes the same counts to both completions.

**Theorem.** These three-site exchanges span $\ker A_r$ over $\mathbb R$.
No bound on $n$ or $m$ is imposed in this mathematical statement.

## 2. Proof by rank-two determinantal ideals

The proof uses a standard algebraic theorem: the ideal generated by the
$t\times t$ minors of a generic matrix over a field is prime. For maximal minors
this is Bruns–Vetter, *Determinantal Rings*, Theorem 2.10; Remark 2.12 extends
the statement to arbitrary $t$, using the perfection result in §5. We use
$t=3$, and the complex Nullstellensatz to identify this radical ideal with the
vanishing ideal of the rank-at-most-two variety. These are explicit external
algebraic dependencies, not claims verified by the Python tests.
[Author-hosted text, printed pp. 14–15](https://www.home.uni-osnabrueck.de/wbruns/brunsw/detrings.pdf),
[book DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0080378).

### Encode a signed coloring law

Introduce a generic $n\times m$ matrix $M$ and put

$$P_h(M)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal K_r}h_\kappa\prod_{i=1}^n M_{i,\kappa(i)}.$$

This polynomial has degree one in each row and degree $r_j$ in column $j$.
The different colorings give different monomials, so $h\mapsto P_h$ is injective.
Substitute $M_{ij}=x_i+y_i c_j$. Expansion by the subset on which a $y_i$ term
is chosen gives

$$P_h(x_i+y_i c_j)
 =\sum_{S,\nu}(A_rh)_{S,\nu}
   \left(\prod_{i\notin S}x_i\right)
   \left(\prod_{i\in S}y_i\right)\prod_j c_j^{\nu_j}.$$

These are distinct monomials in the independent variables $x,y,c$. Thus
$h\in\ker A_r$ if and only if this polynomial is identically zero.

### Why the special form covers generic rank-two matrices

The preceding substitution alone must not be confused with an arbitrary
rank-two factorization. The fixed column degrees provide the missing step.

Take a rank-two matrix $M=VW$, with $V$ having two columns and $W$ two rows,
and assume each column of $W$ is nonzero. Choose a linear functional on
$\mathbb C^2$ that is nonzero on every one of the finitely many columns of $W$.
Such a functional exists, since a finite union of proper linear subspaces
cannot cover $\mathbb C^2$. Complete it to a matrix $T\in GL_2$ and replace
$(V,W)$ by $(VT^{-1},TW)$. The first row of the new $W$ has entries $b_j\ne0$;
write its second row as $d_j$ and the columns of the new $V$ as $x,y$. Then

$$M_{ij}=b_j(x_i+y_i c_j),\qquad c_j=d_j/b_j.$$

Because every coloring uses color $j$ exactly $r_j$ times,

$$P_h(M)=\left(\prod_j b_j^{r_j}\right)P_h(x_i+y_i c_j)=0.$$

This works for **any finite number of colors**, not just three. Rank-two
matrices with nonzero columns form a dense subset of the rank-at-most-two
variety; matrices with zero columns or smaller rank follow by polynomial
continuity. Equivalently, perturb the two factors before taking their product.
Thus $P_h$ vanishes on the whole rank-at-most-two variety.

### Extract the multigraded component

By the determinantal-ideal theorem, write

$$P_h(M)=\sum_{I,J:\,|I|=|J|=3}\det M[I,J]\,Q_{I,J}(M).$$

For exactly three colors these are maximal minors of the transpose; for more
colors they are **all** three-minors. The broader case needs Remark 2.12,
not only the maximal-minor statement.

The polynomial ring and this ideal are graded by all row degrees and all
column degrees. Project the identity onto row degree $(1,\ldots,1)$ and column
degree $(r_0,\ldots,r_{m-1})$. The surviving part of $Q_{I,J}$ has:

- row degree zero on $I$ and one on every row outside $I$;
- column degree $r_j-1$ for $j\in J$ and $r_j$ otherwise.

Its monomials are exactly the outside colorings in the definition of a
three-site exchange. Expanding the determinant supplies their six alternating
completions. Therefore $P_h$, and hence $h$, is a linear combination of the
stated exchanges. If the ideal representation has complex coefficients,
taking real parts gives a real representation because the generators and
$P_h$ have real coefficients. This proves the theorem.

### Degenerate multiplicities and dimensions

A minor using a color with $r_j=0$ would require negative residual column
degree, so it contributes nothing. Only triples of positive-multiplicity
colors survive. In particular, with at most two active colors the kernel is
zero. If $n<3$ or $m<3$, the rank-at-most-two variety is the whole matrix space
and the same injectivity conclusion follows directly. Empty coordinate sets
and zero multiplicities are included. These observations do not silently
assume three nonempty eigenspaces.

## 3. What this says about canonical masses

For a unitary $U$ and color column blocks $J_j$, let

$$r_\kappa(U)=\operatorname{Re}\left(
 \eta(X)\overline{\det U}\prod_j\det U[X_j,J_j]\right),
\qquad X_j=\kappa^{-1}(j).$$

The sign $\eta(X)$ orders the row blocks consecutively. As derived in the
[previous report](../three-color-coupling/README.md#1-canonical-signed-masses-and-a-fixed-correction),
these canonical signed masses sum to one and match every grouped statistic.
Let $H$ have the exchange vectors as columns. The theorem gives the complete
set of matching signed laws:

$$\boxed{\lambda=r(U)+Ht,\qquad t\in\mathbb R^{\#\mathrm{moves}}.}$$

For $(2,2,2)$, one may use the 47 independent directions in
[basis.json](basis.json). The file also gives 47 exact rational coefficients
that reconstruct the earlier positive neighborhood's 90-entry correction.
The replay checks every coordinate, not just a rank count.

This is a real linear spanning theorem. It does not assert integer generation,
a Markov basis, a sequence of individually feasible moves, or convergence of a
greedy algorithm that fixes one negative mass at a time.

## 4. Why color-unitary averaging cannot help

Change orthonormal basis within each color eigenspace:

$$U'=U\operatorname{diag}(V_0,\ldots,V_{m-1}),\qquad V_j\in U(r_j).$$

The block minor for color $j$ gains a factor $\det V_j$, while
$\overline{\det U}$ gains $\overline{\prod_j\det V_j}$. Their product is one.
Consequently **each complex canonical mass, and hence each real canonical
mass, is unchanged pointwise**. Any average over these gauge transformations
has exactly the same masses. The earlier dense six-dimensional fixture has
21 negative canonical real masses, so this is a concrete obstruction to the
proposed averaging repair, not a proof of infeasibility of its pattern law.
Indeed that fixture has a positive corrected law.

Likewise, restricting a repair to colorings with all but two colors fixed gives
no nonzero kernel direction. Within such a fiber, the statistic that every site
of a specified size-$r_j$ set has one selected color identifies a unique coloring.
Thus an individual statistic-preserving local repair must involve at least
three varying colors. This does not prohibit combinations of operations whose
intermediate states change the statistics; such operations are outside the
repair-direction claim.

## 5. The exact positivity cone and its dual

The remaining question is whether $r(U)+Ht\ge0$. By the finite-dimensional
Farkas alternative, exactly one of the following holds:

1. There exists $t$ with $r(U)+Ht\ge0$.
2. There exists $y\ge0$ with $H^Ty=0$ and $y^Tr(U)<0$.

Normalization is automatic because every exchange sums to zero and
$\sum_\kappa r_\kappa(U)=1$. Thus a nonnegative solution is a probability law.
Equivalently, the cone is

$$C=\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{\mathcal K_r}+\operatorname{im}H,
\qquad C^*=\{y\ge0:H^Ty=0\},$$

and feasibility is $r(U)\in C$. The cone is polyhedral, so the separating
alternative has no closure gap. One may normalize a nonzero dual by
$\sum_\kappa y_\kappa=1$. For $(2,2,2)$, $H$ has rank 47 in 90 coordinates;
$\ker H^T$ has dimension 43 and the normalized dual polytope has affine
dimension 42. The strictly positive constant vector lies in that subspace,
so the nonnegative inequalities do not reduce its affine hull.

The condition $H^Ty=0$ says that every local six-term alternating sum of the
coloring cost $y$ vanishes. It is equivalent to $y$ belonging to the row space
of $A_r$. These are all **linear** restrictions on potential dual costs;
$y\ge0$ is the cone restriction. Their expectation at a particular unitary,
$y^Tr(U)$, is a further substantive polynomial inequality. The exchange theorem
does not establish its sign.

### Ordinary MDO tests only a special family of dual costs

For spectra $a_i$, color values $\beta_j$, and a complex direction $\zeta$,
set

$$v_\kappa=\prod_i(a_i+\beta_{\kappa(i)}),\quad
M=\max_\kappa\operatorname{Re}(\zeta v_\kappa),\quad
y_\kappa=M-\operatorname{Re}(\zeta v_\kappa).$$

Then $y\ge0$ and $H^Ty=0$. On any exchange, the alternating vertex sum is an
outside factor times a determinant with entries $a_{i_p}+\beta_{j_q}$,
a matrix of rank at most two. Therefore that sum vanishes. For canonical
masses, Cauchy–Binet gives

$$y^Tr(U)=M-\operatorname{Re}\left(\zeta\det(D_a+UD_\beta U^*)\right).$$

An original determinant hull violation would give a negative cost in this
special product-spectral family. A general negative element of $C^*$ need not
have this form. No assertion that these costs generate all of $C^*$ is made.
This separates the complete linear relations from the restricted directions
available to a single original spectral support test.

### A known four-color obstruction calibrates the distinction

For pattern $(1,1,1,1)$ there are 24 colorings. The 16 exchanges span a
10-dimensional kernel; the statistic map has rank 14. For the archived
[four-cycle matrix](../single-cycle/README.md#5-a-four-cycle-with-no-common-minor-certificate),
let $h_\kappa$ be its 13-term permutation-cover score and put $y=h-1$.
The exact replay checks

$$y\ge0,\qquad H^Ty=0,\qquad y^Tr(U)=-672/15625<0.$$

Thus the generators are complete even in a case where positivity is impossible.
This reuses a known repository obstruction; it is not a new MDO counterexample.

## 6. Exact replay and remaining research

Python 3.9+ and the standard library only. From the repository root:

```sh
python3 -B research/marcus-de-oliveira/three-color-exchanges/exchanges.py
python3 -B -m unittest discover -s research/marcus-de-oliveira/three-color-exchanges -p 'test_*.py' -v
```

The first command matches [verification.json](verification.json). Optional
`--derive` regenerates [basis.json](basis.json) by exact elimination. The check
constructs all statistic and exchange rows, proves their orthogonality, computes
both ranks over rational numbers, reconstructs all 90 published correction
coordinates, and verifies three nontrivial complex color-unitary rotations.
It also checks zero/smaller multiplicities and the four-color dual above.
Eight tests exercise regeneration, changed coefficients, missing basis entries,
non-kernel vectors, invalid/inexact inputs, and the explicit cone separator.
No floating rank tolerance, linear optimizer, or random trial is used.

The finite executable is intentionally bounded to dimension seven; the written
algebraic theorem has no such bound. The rank calculations corroborate specific
instances and do not replace the determinantal-ideal proof. Local validation
used Python 3.9.6. The author-hosted Bruns–Vetter PDF was downloaded and its
Theorem 2.10 and Remark 2.12 read directly (printed pp. 14–15); the book is not
copied into the repository.
