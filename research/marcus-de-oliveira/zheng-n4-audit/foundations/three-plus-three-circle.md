## 1. Statement

Notation as in the brief: x = alpha * (matching vector of v_1..v_8 in C^2), F_sigma(x) = alpha prod_i [A_i, C_sigma(i)], with A_i = a_i and C_j = -b_j as points of P^1(C) (the lib convention v_{4+j} = (-b_j, 1)), c(x) = Re(x - F_{pi*}(x) kappa), and Phi as in (3.4).

**Theorem (3+3).** Let x in X \ {0} have a representation by points A_1..A_4, C_1..C_4 of P^1(C) such that for some I, J in {1,2,3,4} with |I| = |J| = 3 the six points A_i (i in I) and C_j (j in J) lie on one generalized circle (circle or line). Coincidences among any of the eight points are allowed, and the two remaining points, the direction alpha in C* and the reference pi* are arbitrary. Then P c(x) >= 0 implies Phi_{c(x)}(O) >= 0 for all O in SO(4).

**Stronger form proved.** Let K = cone(34 minor vectors, 12 atoms), the atoms being the 12 stored vectors, each a G-image of f70 + 2 f2 + f12. There is g in G (a row permutation and a column permutation) such that every c in the real 5-dimensional profile space L of the configuration (defined in Section 2) with Pc >= 0 lies in g.K. Here g.K is generated by minors and by G-images of the master atom.

Symmetry variants: all 4 x 4 choices of (I, J) are covered, by a row permutation and a column permutation in G. A circle condition on three a's and three -b's is the only form used. The transpose part of G is not needed.

## 2. Proof

**Step 0. The generators of K and their G-images have Phi >= 0 on SO(4).**
- Minors: Phi_{n_{I,J}}(O) = det O[I,J]^2 (4.2) (lib README, PROOF-READ there, EXACT on 24 rational points).
- Master atom m = f70 + 2 f2 + f12, with f2 = n_{13,34}, f12 = n_{14,24}, f70 = h_{4231,+} (EXACT from lib's Appendix D table). Put x2 = det O[13,34] and x3 = det O[14,24]. Then Phi_m = Phi(f2+f70) + x2^2 + x3^2 >= -2|x2 x3| + x2^2 + x3^2 = (|x2| - |x3|)^2 >= 0 by (E1).
  - (E1) proof (gap check, Steps 1-5): write O = (x -> p x conj q) and P = Wp, Q = Wq, v_i = P_i^2 - Q_i^2. The key identity is Phi(f2+f70) = 2[delta^2 + Delta^2 - (v0 v2 + v1 v3)], with x2 = v0 + v1 and x3 = v1 + v2. I re-derived it with my own sympy code, homogenized by |p|^2 and |q|^2 (EXACT; stage `atoms`, signs +,+). The rest is a triangle inequality plus sqrt((r+u)(s+v)) - sqrt(rs) >= sqrt(uv) in the two sign cases. I re-read each step; it is correct (PROOF-READ).
  - "T34 master at tau = 1": m is exactly the endpoint inequality (EP_b) of condition (H) of the T_34 note, at d' = 1, b = 1, a' = 2 = d' + d'^2/b.
- G-invariance (PROOF-READ, short). Take g: sigma -> rho sigma rho' and let O' be the permuted matrix O'_{i,k} = O_{rho'(i), rho^{-1}(k)}. Substituting gives Phi_{g.c}(O) = sgn(rho rho') Phi_c(O'), with det O' = sgn(rho rho') det O. If the sign is -1, replace O' by O'' = O' diag(-1,1,1,1). Every Leibniz product uses exactly one entry of column 1, so this flips all 24 products, and O'' is in SO(4). The inverse sigma -> sigma^{-1} corresponds to O -> O^T. Hence {c : Phi_c >= 0 on SO(4)} is G-invariant, and all 288 elements of the orbit of m are valid. The supplement's base atom 2 n_{12,34} + n_{14,23} + h_{3421,-} and all 12 stored atoms lie in this orbit (EXACT).

**Step 1. Moebius reduction.** For T in GL_2(C), [Tu, Tv] = det T [u, v]. So moving all eight points by one Moebius map, and rescaling representatives, multiplies the matching vector by one nonzero complex constant mu. Then x = alpha m(points) = (alpha/mu) m(T points), and c(x) and the hypothesis P c(x) >= 0 are unchanged. Choose T mapping the circle to R ∪ {∞}.

**Step 2. Relabelling.** Relabelling A_i (C_j) by a permutation moves F_sigma to F_{sigma rho^{-1}} (resp. F_{rho sigma}), which is the G-action. By Step 0 this preserves validity. So assume I = J = {1,2,3}. The free points are A_4 = z and C_4 = w, arbitrary in P^1(C).

**Step 3. Profile space.** Write z, w homogeneously. The matching vector is bilinear in (z, w), so x = alpha sum_{a,b} z_a w_b X_ab(Γ). The X_ab(Γ) are real, because the six points Γ are real. Hence Re(alpha x) = sum Re(alpha z_a w_b) X_ab, and

  c(x) ∈ L(Γ) := span_R{X_11, X_12, X_21, X_22, kappa}   (dimension <= 5).

For Γ = (A_1, A_2, A_3) = (∞, 0, 1), (C_1, C_2, C_3) = (s, t, u), one finds F_sigma = -C_sigma(2) (1 - C_sigma(3)) (zeta - C_sigma(4)), with C_4 = eta. This is multi-affine, and its four coefficient columns in (1, zeta, eta, zeta eta) are P-images of real vectors, identically in s, t, u (EXACT, stage `chambers`). L(Γ) is unchanged by real Moebius maps (Step 1 with det T real, and z, w ranging over all of C^2).

**Step 4. Duality, with complete vertex lists.** Facts: kappa = (1/7) sum of minors, and the minors have rank 14 (EXACT). So kappa is interior to K, and R' = {w : w(kappa) = 1, w >= 0 on the generators} is a polytope. If c is not in K, separation gives w in R' with w(c) < 0, hence a vertex with w(c) < 0. So c ∈ K iff w(c) >= 0 at every vertex of R'. The same holds for K_0 = cone(minors) and R.
- My own exact double description (integer arithmetic, combinatorial adjacency test) gives exactly the 72 stored vertices of R and exactly the 86 stored vertices of R' (EXACT).
- Each stored vertex is feasible and has an active set of rank 13 (EXACT).
- 400 random LPs per polytope agree to 3e-15 (NUMERIC cross-check of completeness).
- The 72 vertices are the 24 evaluations v_sigma plus 48 others; the atoms cut off exactly 2 of the 48 (indices 45 and 65 of the 72-list).

**Step 5. Chamber certificates.** Let Γ be generic: three distinct A's, three distinct C's, no C equal to an A. Map (A_1, A_2, A_3) to (∞, 0, 1) by a real Moebius map. The C's are then three distinct reals off {0, 1}. The Moebius maps permuting {0, 1, ∞}, with the matching relabelling of A_1..A_3, act on the three arcs (-∞,0), (0,1), (1,∞) as the full S_3: 1 - x and 1/x are two transpositions. Sorting the C's is a column relabelling. So every generic Γ is G-equivalent to one of three chambers, parametrized bijectively by x, y, z > 0:
- I: 0 < s < t < u < 1, with s = x/(1+x+y+z), t = (x+y)/(...), u = (x+y+z)/(...). Pattern AAACCC.
- II: 0 < s < t < 1 < u, with s = x/(1+x+y), t = (x+y)/(1+x+y), u = 1+z. Pattern AACCAC.
- III: s < 0 < t < 1 < u, with s = -x, t = y/(1+y), u = 1+z. Pattern ACACAC.

For each non-ordinary vertex w (48 in I and II with R; 62 in III with R'), the stored record gives polynomials R, W_sigma in x, y, z with nonnegative coefficients, R ≠ 0. They satisfy sum_sigma W_sigma = R (the kappa column, since w(kappa) = v_sigma(kappa) = 1), and for each of the four columns k, sum_sigma W_sigma v_sigma(B_k) = R w(B_k) after clearing the positive denominator. I replayed all 158 records exactly against my own F_sigma (EXACT). A negative control, one record tested against a wrong vertex, is rejected in each chamber.

Since R > 0 on the open orthant, w(c) = sum (W_sigma/R) v_sigma(c) >= 0 for every c in L(Γ) with Pc >= 0. Ordinary vertices are trivial. By Step 4, c ∈ K_0 in chambers I and II, and c ∈ K in chamber III. Minors alone fail in chamber III (N3).

**Step 6. Uniformity and degenerate configurations (the key step).**
- The certificate is uniform because L(Γ) is spanned by four columns that are polynomial in (s,t,u), and the identities are polynomial identities on the whole open chamber, not finitely many samples.
- For non-generic Γ (coincidences; also a C at the image of an A, or A's coinciding), use closure. Parametrize Γ by unit representatives in (S^1)^6. The X_ab(Γ) are continuous, multilinear. For fixed g and a vertex w of the dual of g.K, the condition "w restricted to L(Γ) lies in cone(v_sigma restricted to L(Γ))" is equivalent to the existence of lambda in the simplex Δ_23 with (sum lambda_sigma v_sigma - w)(X_ab(Γ)) = 0 for all a, b. The weights sum to 1 because kappa ∈ L(Γ). By compactness of Δ_23 this condition is closed in Γ.
- Generic Γ are dense and fall into finitely many classes Ω_g, each satisfying the condition for its g. So every Γ lies in some closure cl(Ω_g) and satisfies the condition for that g. By Farkas, every c ∈ L(Γ) with Pc >= 0 lies in g.K.
- This covers coincident points, collisions A_i = C_j, and the points at infinity, with no separate case analysis. The closure argument is needed and works because the conclusion is about the closed cone, not about strictness of gaps.

Step 0 then gives Phi_{c(x)} >= 0. ∎

## 5. Numerical attack (seed 20261125; quaternion chart of SO(4); BFGS)

- N1: 4,000 realizable configurations. Each has six points on a random circle (Moebius image of random reals; every 5th has a near-coincidence at 1e-3), random free slots (I, J random), random complex free points and a random direction, with reference = argmin. This test is weak: generic configurations are deep inside.
- N2: the full profile family. At 44 chamber parameters (12 random per chamber, plus 8 closure and degenerate points such as s = t, s = 0, u = 1, t = u = 0.5), I enumerated all extreme rays of {c ∈ L : Pc >= 0}, 33,347 rays in total. The worst value of w(c) over the chamber's vertex list was -4.0e-12 (I), -7.0e-15 (II) and -7.2e-15 (III). Min Phi on a subset was -1.1e-15. This tests the stronger structural claim on all of L, not only realizable points.

- N4 (control): 500 generic non-concyclic configurations with a random direction were all inside the cone too. So random realizable sampling hardly stresses the claim, and N2/N3 carry the weight of the numerical evidence.
