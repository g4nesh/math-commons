# MDO-MINORS · Which three-value spectra admit common weights?

**Both spectra paired, all relative unitaries.** A written proof now gives 21 nonnegative weights summing to one and depending on $U$ alone, valid simultaneously for every $a,b\in\mathbb C^3$ when each entry is repeated twice. The triples may have coincident entries. The complete 189-ray classification reduces the problem to four additional physical signs, all proved by a Grassmann compression: 24 exact leading-minor polynomials have positive coefficients and even exponents. Four complex-frame fixtures check the 720-term convention and all 141 polynomial coefficients. This resolves the two-sided $(2,2,2)\times(2,2,2)$ case; independent-owner mathematical review is pending. It is an exact algebraic proof, separate from the Lean forest results. **Corollary in dimensions at most six.** The written results cover MDO whenever **each** matrix has at most three distinct eigenvalues, at any multiplicities. If either side has at most two values, or three with a singleton, reviewed Theorem D allows an arbitrary opposite spectrum. Otherwise, three positive multiplicities each at least two sum to at most six only as $(2,2,2)$; the new two-sided theorem applies. This corollary inherits the pending independent review of that new step. It does not include $(2,2,2)$ opposite four or more distinct values.

**Theorem D passed independent mathematical review.** Theorem D gives inclusion if either matrix has at most two distinct eigenvalues, or exactly three with at least one simple. Its distribution works across the spectra at fixed relative unitary and pattern. The author flags possible prior-art overlap with Drury (1994). The reviewer checked the coupling and marginal identities and requested corrections to the dimension-four search summary. The subsequent re-review requested three minor reporting changes, and the authors applied them. **One paired spectrum and an arbitrary opposite spectrum.** For three eigenvalues each of multiplicity two, forgetting the eigenvalue labels gives perfect-matching marginals for every relative unitary. On the relative boundary, the labels can always be restored, giving one distribution that works for all complex eigenvalues of both matrices. Color symmetry gives interior neighborhoods, and a residual matching test also certifies some nonsymmetric unitaries. Four fixtures each replay all 358 grouped coefficients. For an unrestricted opposite spectrum, the remaining question is whether all interior points coming from unitaries admit the required joint labeling. **Five triple correlations for the one-sided problem.** For an arbitrary relative unitary with one $(2,2,2)$ spectrum and an unrestricted opposite spectrum, the 45 colored-edge probabilities together with five alternating triple correlations determine all 358 grouped coefficients. The edge data have rank 38; the full data have rank 43. An explicit signed correction preserves every colored edge, and under cyclic color symmetry the remaining positivity question becomes a necessary-and-sufficient test on 15 real orientation variables. **All complex qubit Fourier bases.** For $U=(W_0\oplus W_1\oplus W_2)(F_3\otimes I_2)$ with arbitrary complex unitary $2\times2$ blocks, one common coloring law now works for every opposing complex spectrum and every choice of the three paired eigenvalues. The written Pauli calculation places every physical axis triple in a bounded four-dimensional polytope; exact nonnegative lifts at all 66 vertices reproduce all 358 grouped coefficients. The checker and six tests include three complex unitary fixtures. This covers the whole displayed family, including degenerate axes, while the arbitrary-$U(6)$ one-sided case with an unrestricted opposite spectrum remains open. Independent-owner review is pending. **Retained constructive cases and limits.** Symplectic bases and their neighborhoods have a Pfaffian construction. Triangular half-product hulls give an explicit spectral neighborhood of radius $1/16384$, and an exact pentagonal example has eight positive edge certificates plus rank-13 face calculations proving a qualitative open neighborhood for every relative unitary. These two-sided domains are now subsumed by the universal theorem but retain explicit alternative certificates. The 45 elementary inequalities alone still fail for some abstract pointwise data; that obstruction is not a physical counterexample.
