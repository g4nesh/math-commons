# Preserving multiplicities in the contact reduction

Fix positive left multiplicities $r_1,\ldots,r_R$ and right multiplicities $s_1,\ldots,s_C$ of the same total $n$, with $R,C\ge2$. Write $\mathcal T$ for all nonnegative integer $R\times C$ tables with these margins. The different indexed products in this pattern are
$$z_M(a,b)=\prod_{ij}(a_i+b_j)^{M_{ij}},\qquad M\in\mathcal T.$$

**Theorem.** Fix the relative unitary $U$. If some spectra with these multiplicities violate the Marcus–de Oliveira inclusion, there are spectra in the **same grouped parameter family** for the same $U$ and a separating supporting line having at least $R+C-2$ distinct **tables** in contact. The output can be taken isolated in the complex level set of its contact gaps and their total, as defined below. The normalized violation can be kept at least as strong. Different tables can give the same complex point, and eigenvalue values in different groups may coincide at the endpoint.

With one unconstrained six-entry spectrum and one $(2,2,2)$ spectrum the count is **seven of 90**. With both spectra $(2,2,2)$ it is **four of 21**. Counting individual six-coordinate permutations would give redundant contacts automatically and could change the multiplicities during deformation. The grouped theorem imposes the original spectral pattern throughout.

We extend Ishaan's all-dimensional contact theorem. Its complex-curve maximum principle is used below with its original assumptions; the weighted configuration space, dimension count, transportation-table obstruction and divisible-pattern improvement are proved here. [Original all-dimensional proof](../n5-contact-program/contact-reduction/README.md)

## 1. Coefficients and the grouped variety

The result holds for fixed real weights $\mu_M$ summing to one if the first-moment table $A=\sum_M\mu_M M$ is entrywise nonnegative. For a unitary, aggregate the real signed Leibniz weights by table. Then $A_{ij}$ is the sum of $|U_{kl}|^2$ over the row block $i$ and column block $j$. The expected determinant is $\sum_M\mu_M z_M$ for every choice of the grouped values by expansion of $\det(D_a U+UD_b)$. If the greatest common divisor of all the margins is at least two, the theorem even holds for **every** real $\mu$ summing to one.

Use two-vectors $v_i,w_j\in\mathbb C^2$ and brackets $[v_i,w_j]=\det(v_i,w_j)$. Define $\Phi_M=\prod[v_i,w_j]^{M_{ij}}$ and $X=\mathrm{im}\,\Phi$. It is a complex cone, since multiplying one vector by a scalar multiplies all products by the same positive power of that scalar, and complex roots exist. The vector of ones belongs to $X$. At any nonzero point all two-vectors are nonzero; a common $\mathrm{SL}_2$ change puts each into the affine chart with nonzero second coordinate. Thus every nonzero point of $X$ is a nonzero complex scalar times some $z(a,b)$ with the specified groups.

**Closedness.** Apply common $\mathrm{SL}_2$ changes and individual nonzero scalar changes $\lambda_i,\gamma_j$ with $\prod_i\lambda_i^{r_i}\prod_j\gamma_j^{s_j}=1$. These preserve every $\Phi_M$. On the closure of an orbit with nonzero image minimize the squared norm weighted by $r_i,s_j$. Diagonal real scalings with zero weighted logarithmic sum show all the two-vectors at a minimizer have the same length $h>0$. Trace-zero Hermitian variations of the common $\mathrm{SL}_2$ change give
$$\sum_i r_i v_i v_i^*+\sum_j s_j w_j w_j^*=nh^2 I_2.$$
The minimizer exists because the norm is proper and zero is excluded by continuity of the invariant image. On the unit-length balanced locus any line contains at most $n$ vectors counted with multiplicity, by evaluating the matrix identity on a unit vector on that line. If all matching products vanished, expand $v_i,w_j$ into identical copies with their respective multiplicities. Hall's theorem would give a zero-bracket rectangle with at least $n+1$ total rows and columns. Its nonzero vectors would all lie on one line, a contradiction. Thus $\Phi$ is nonzero on this compact normalized locus. The image is its positive radial cone together with zero, and is Euclidean-closed by compactness and a positive lower bound on $\|\Phi\|$. It is irreducible and Zariski-closed by constructibility and classical density of a nonempty Zariski-open set in its irreducible closure, as in the source's Lemma D.

**Dimension.** In the generic projective chart take the first two left points at $0,\infty$ and the first right point at $1$. For any distinct two rows and two columns there is a table with one unit in the two prescribed diagonal cells: prescribe them first and fill the remaining integer margins greedily. Moving one unit from each diagonal to each antidiagonal gives another table. The ratio of their coordinates on $X$ is exactly the corresponding bracket cross-ratio. Using the first two left points and the first right point recovers each remaining right coordinate. Using the first left point and the first two right points then recovers each remaining left coordinate. These statements are rational on the open set of distinct points; for $0,\infty;1,q$ the chosen ratio is $1/q$, and for $0,p;1,q$ it is $(p-q)/(q(p-1))$. The ratios recover $R+C-3$ independent projective parameters. One additional nonzero product determines an independent common scale. Conversely these projective parameters and scale parametrize the generic image. Therefore $\dim_\mathbb C X=R+C-2$.

## 2. Lines in the common translation direction

Suppose $x+\mathbb C\mathbf1\subset X$. For every integer relation $\sum_M c_M M=0$, the monomial identity $\prod_M\Phi_M^{c_M^+}=\prod_M\Phi_M^{c_M^-}$ holds. By conicity $1+t x$ satisfies it for every $t$. Differentiating at zero gives $\sum c_M x_M=0$. The integer kernel spans the complex kernel of an integer matrix, so $x_M=\langle Q,M\rangle$ for some complex matrix $Q$. The first-moment table $A$ has margins $r,s$. When it is nonnegative, expand it into the $n\times n$ matrix whose block $ij$ is constant $A_{ij}/(r_i s_j)$. This matrix is doubly stochastic. Birkhoff decomposition followed by aggregation gives $A=\sum_M\beta_M M$ with nonnegative $\beta$ summing to one. Thus $\sum_M\mu_M x_M=\langle Q,A\rangle=\sum_M\beta_M x_M$ lies in the hull, precluding a violation anywhere on this type of line at $x$.

**Divisible margins.** Write $r=d r',s=d s'$ with $d\ge2$. Every table $M$ decomposes into $d$ integer tables with margins $r',s'$. To see this, route one layer through a bipartite network with supplies $r'$, demands $s'$, and edge capacities $M_{ij}$. The fractional routing $M/d$ is feasible. Integral max flow gives one integer layer, and induction gives the rest. For a configuration representing $x$, write $p_N$ for each smaller-table product. The full coordinates include $p_N^d,p_N^{d-1}p_L,p_N^{d-2}p_L^2$ for every pair $N,L$. In $X$ the first times the third always equals the second squared. On $x+t\mathbf1$, the coefficient of $t$ gives $p_N^{d-2}(p_N-p_L)^2=0$. If $x\ne0$, some $p_N$ is nonzero by decomposition into layers. Fix it to obtain $p_L=p_N$ for all $L$. Every full product is therefore the same. Such a point cannot violate the inclusion for any normalized signed weights. This replaces the first-moment assumption entirely in the divisible case.

## 3. Deformation and counted contacts

Rotate and scale any strict separation so a reference $\pi\in\mathcal T$ minimizes the real coordinate, all $g_M=x_M-x_\pi$ have nonnegative real parts, and $\sum_M\Re g_M=1$. This sum can only vanish if the normalized signed expectation has no strict violation. Write $T=\{M:\Re g_M=0\}$ and $L(x)=\sum_M\mu_M\Re g_M<0$. On the affine slice $\Lambda$ freeze the **complex** $g_M$ for $M\in T$ and the complex sum of all $g_M$.

Suppose a positive-dimensional irreducible component through $x$ remains in $X\cap\Lambda$. If all gaps are constant on it, it is the whole line $x+\mathbb C\mathbf1$, excluded by Section 2. Otherwise an irreducible affine curve through $x$ and a point where one gap differs has a nonconstant gap. On its normalization, the gaps are holomorphic functions meromorphic at finitely many missing points of a compact connected Riemann surface. In the region where noncontact gaps have strictly positive real parts, their real parts sum to one. Hence $L$ is bounded there. The maximum-principle argument of Lemmas C and E in the source applies: a boundary point has one more real contact, all gaps nonnegative, and $L$ no larger than before. If $L$ is nonconstant along the curve first choose an interior point with smaller $L$ and an intermediate sublevel. This yields strict decrease in that case. The frozen contacts and normalization survive. [Detailed maximum principle and deformation step](../n5-contact-program/contact-reduction/README.md#4-a-maximum-principle-on-a-punctured-riemann-surface)

The contact set strictly grows, so iteration ends at a point isolated in $X\cap\Lambda$. The reference contributes no gap equation, hence $\Lambda$ has at most $|T|$ defining complex equations including the sum. Every component of this intersection has dimension at least $\dim X-|T|$. The isolated point therefore has $|T|\ge R+C-2$. Returning through the shared affine chart preserves the supporting line up to rotation and positive scale.

## 4. A nonvacuous exact example for admissible signed data

Use the parity-ordered permutations $012,120,201,021,102,210$ of the three blocks. Write $p_\pi=\prod_r(a_r+b_{\pi(r)})$. For two $(2,2,2)$ spectra the 21 products are $p_i p_j$ with $i\le j$. Use the fixed signed expectation $D=2p_0p_1-p_1^2$. It sums to one as a signed law, and its first-moment table is $2P_0\ge0$. These weights illustrate the abstract theorem. They are not claimed to come from a unitary.

Take $a=(t/10,t,1+t)$ and $b=(0,1,1+2t)$ for $0\le t\le1$. All products are nonnegative. Here $p_0=(t/10)(1+t)(2+3t)$ and $p_1=(1+t/10)(1+3t)(1+t)$. The difference $p_1-2p_0=(1+t)(1+27t/10-3t^2/10)$ is positive throughout, so $D<0$. At $t=1$ there is one contact table; at $t=0$ there are 18. The normalized violation is strictly stronger at the endpoint in the exact replay.

The endpoint is rigid. Only $p_1$ and $p_5$ are nonzero there. On the frozen contact slice all 18 contact coordinates have a common value $k$, since their complex differences are zero. For any of the four vanished indices $i$, the equations include $p_i^2=p_i p_j=k$ for every $j$. If $k\ne0$ all $p_j$ would coincide, contradicting the normalized gap sum. Thus $k=0$ and those four $p_i$ vanish. The determinant identity $\sum_i\mathrm{sgn}(i)p_i=0$ forces the remaining even and odd products $p_1,p_5$ to agree. The complex total of the gaps fixes all three nonzero table coordinates. In the homogeneous setting the same argument uses square roots of the common scale, available over $\mathbb C$. The level set on $X$ therefore contains just this endpoint.
