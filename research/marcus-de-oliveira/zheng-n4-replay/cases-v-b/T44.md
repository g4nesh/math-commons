## Statement and scope

Case $T_{44}$: if $x\in X$ and $c(x)\in N_{T_{44}}$, then $\Phi_{c(x)}(O)\ge0$ for every $O\in\mathrm{SO}(4)$.

- **Non-strict contacts.** The argument uses only $c(x)\in N_T$. The cone has ten generators and is not simplicial; the coordinate $d$ can be negative, and the whole part $d\le0$ is treated (step 4). For $d>0$ zero coefficients, hence further contacts, are allowed in every step (steps 16–17 treat every face). PROOF-READ.
- **Every $O\in\mathrm{SO}(4)$.** The conclusion is that $c(x)$ is a nonnegative combination of minor vectors, or satisfies a capacity that holds on all of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$.
- **All spectra.** The proof never parametrizes by spectra. It uses only $x\in X$, through $X\subset\{\nabla C=0\}$ (EXACT) and the two equations of PROOF.md 7.3. So repeated eigenvalues and $a_i+b_j=0$ are covered by the same argument, and nothing is delegated to Appendix B or Theorem C.1. PROOF-READ.

Logical shape. If $d\le0$, $c(x)$ is a minor combination. If $d>0$ (normalized to $d=1$) and the old-H capacity fails, then $C_0>0$, so $Q\ne0$ and the radial equations force $G_9=0$. If also the four-coefficient capacity fails, then $k_0=G_9\vert_{c=0}>0$, and for the region of $(a,b,e,j)$ at hand the document names an endpoint $J$ that is the threshold of a capacity "$c\ge J$" and shows $G_9>0$ on $0\le c<J$. Hence $c\ge J$ and that capacity gives $\Phi\ge0$.
