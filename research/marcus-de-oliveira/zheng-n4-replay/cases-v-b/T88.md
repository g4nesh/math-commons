## Statement and scope

Case $T_{88}$: if $x\in X$ and $c(x)\in N_{T_{88}}$, then $\Phi_{c(x)}(O)\ge0$ for every $O\in\mathrm{SO}(4)$.

- **Non-strict contacts.** The argument uses only that the 24 gaps are $\ge0$ and vanish on $T$. Zero coefficients, hence additional contacts, are allowed in every step; the charts are closed in all optional parameters.
- **Every $O\in\mathrm{SO}(4)$.** The conclusion is that $c(x)$ (or its transpose, see step 5) is a nonnegative combination of squared-minor vectors and of three combinations covered by the SOC inequality, which holds on all of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$.
- **All spectra.** The proof never parametrizes by spectra and uses no normalising difference of products. It uses only that $x\in X$, through $X\subset\{\nabla C=0\}$ (EXACT) and the two equations of PROOF.md 7.3. So repeated eigenvalues and $a_i+b_j=0$ are covered by the same argument, and nothing is delegated to Appendix B or Theorem C.1. PROOF-READ.
- **No use of the 82-facet theorem.** The eight vectors are a basis of the solution space of the six contact equations, seven coefficients are gaps, and the eighth is a difference of two gaps, whose two signs are both treated. So Proposition 6.4 and Lemma 6.5 are not needed.

The logical shape: if $d<0$, the inversion $\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}$ turns the gap vector into one of the same form with $d>0$. If $d=0$ only squared-minor vectors occur. If $d>0$ (normalized to $d=1$), the SOC inequality gives $\Phi\ge0$ when $H_3(a,b,c)\ge1$. At a point of $X$ the two radial equations force $G=0$ for an explicit polynomial $G$. The document shows $G>0$ at every point of the closed orthant with $H_3(a,b,c)<1$. Hence $H_3\ge1$.
