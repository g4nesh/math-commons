### Boundaries (steps 16 and 23)

**First branch.** For each $(a,b,e,j)$ with $K<0$ one needs a threshold $J$ that is valid (safe for $c\ge J$) and for which the two Bernstein inequalities hold; then (9) excludes $c<J$. On a chart point this is step 15. At a boundary point that is a limit of chart points along which $J$ converges to a finite positive number, the Bernstein inequalities pass to the limit, $k_0>0$ by (8), and the limit threshold is valid because the set of coefficient vectors with $\Phi\ge0$ on $\mathrm{SO}(4)$ is closed. I checked every layer the document lists:

- $e=j=0$: no threshold exists; the explicit product is positive for all $c\ge0$ (EXACT).
- $e=0<j$: rows I with $V=0$. $j=0<e$: rows II, III with $U=0$.
- $j=e$ and the $j$-cuts: $U=0$ of the SOC rows.
- $a=0<b$ with $j\ge e$: exchange by the stabilizer (step 13), then row I$^-$ with $T=0$. $a=b=0$: limit of row I$^-$, threshold $1/j$ unchanged; with $j<e$: row IIIa with $T=0$.
- $a=b<1$ with $j<e$: limit $V\to\infty$ of IIIa; the thresholds there do not depend on $a,b$.
- $b=1$: IIIb with $T=0$ (the parallel term equals 1 there, the old-H threshold; identity checked).
- $a=0$, $b\ge1$, $j<e$: IIIb with $V=0$. The parallel term equals $1/b$ there (identity checked), so the threshold is $1/e+1/b$; it is covered by C5(ii) at $a=0$ and is also the ordinary chain condition, since $(45;5,0;35)$ is in the chain orbit (EXACT).
- $ab=1$: for $j\ge e$ limit of row I$^-$; for $j<e$ limit of row II with $J\to1/e$, valid by the cited double-SOC bound (or by closedness).
- $e\to E_{\max}$: then $K\to0$, outside the hard set.

Exact evaluation at 2200 rational points assigned to rows from the original coordinates, with all these layers present, found no violation (test 1).

**Second branch.** Tables need $p,q\ge0$ and $b,c>0$ ($T,L>0$). For $b=0$ or $c=0$ the document replaces zeros by $\varepsilon$ and lets $\varepsilon\to0$: $J_\varepsilon$ increases to the extended minimum $J$ (monotonicity in the coefficients, compactness of the feasible set and joint lower semicontinuity of $x^2/t$ with $0/0=0$); (14) passes to the limit with $1/J=0$ if $J=\infty$; and for finite $J$ the costs with central coefficient $J_\varepsilon$ are safe and converge, so $a\ge J$ is safe by closedness. I read this argument and found no gap. Exact evaluation with the closed forms of $J$ at $b=0<c$, $c=0<b$ and $b=c=0$ (554 rational points, test 3) found no violation. $as=1$ is the closed SOC bound.

## Observations

- **Row II fraction.** The document's term counts 9616 and 16507 for row II are those of $J=(E_{\max}-e)/(eE_{\max})$ written over the product of the two reduced numerators, which carries the common factor $(A+1)(A+T+1)$. With the reduced fraction $J=AT/(V(A+1)(A+T+1))$ the two numerators have 6832 and 9514 terms and are also coefficientwise nonnegative. Both are checked.
- **The printed permutation triples can be decoded.** Without transpose the document's (row, column) is $(\rho'^{-1},\rho)$ in the convention $\sigma\mapsto\rho\sigma\rho'$; with transpose it is $(\rho,\rho')$ with $\sigma\mapsto\rho\sigma^{-1}\rho'$. All four printed actions match.
- **Feasibility is enough for the energy.** The proof needs each chart energy only as the value of (10) at a feasible allocation (so that $a\ge J$ is safe) together with coverage of all positive $(p,b,c,q)$ by the charts. Optimality of the charts is true but not needed.
- **The parallel bound is used only for $b\ge1$.** There it never exceeds the chain threshold $1/e+1/b$ (identity in step 16), which is why the cut of $j$ at $eb/(e+b)$ is a valid, non-optimal choice.
- Both hard branches are realized by points of $X$ (numerically). In the first branch some realizable points lie in rows II and IIIb$^-$, where the selected threshold is one of the cited bounds C5.
