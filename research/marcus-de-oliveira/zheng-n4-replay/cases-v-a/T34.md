## What the document proves, in outline

Write g = c(x) = p·f₀ + r·f₁ + a·f₂ + b·f₁₂ + c·f₃₉ + q·f₆₇ + d·f₇₀ + s·f₇₂ with all coefficients ≥ 0. If d = 0, g is a combination of minor vectors. For d > 0:

1. **Three sufficient conditions** for Φ_g ≥ 0 on SO(4): (H) a ≥ d + d²/(b+c); (PB) a ≥ d²/p + d²/b; (CQ) a ≥ d²/c + d²/q (a zero denominator means the condition is void).
2. **Radial equation.** With v the lift of g to V_ℝ, C(v + Hκ) = C + HQ for two explicit polynomials C, Q in the coefficients. Radial necessity gives a real H with C + HQ = 0 and det Hess C(v + Hκ) = 0. For d > 0 one shows Q ≠ 0, so H = −C/Q, and Q²·det Hess C(v − (C/Q)κ) = −4·R₉² for a polynomial R₉ of degree 9. Hence R₉ = 0.
3. **Polynomial implication.** For nonnegative coefficients with d > 0, R₉ = 0 forces (H) or (PB) or (CQ). After d = 1, R₉ is a quadratic in a; its Bernstein coefficients on [0, min(A₀, A₁)] are shown nonnegative by twelve coefficient-sign certificates on three rational charts, for each of the two orders p ≥ q and q ≥ p.

## The chain Gram identity (task item b)

PROOF.md 7.6 calls the inequality behind (PB) and (CQ) the *chain* inequality, Φ₇₀ + 2\|x₂\|(\|x₀\| + \|x₁₂\|) ≥ 0, and notes that no public PDF proves it. The document (p. 1) describes the two Gram matrices and says the identities are in its verifier.

Verified here, exactly, with own code:

- **Rebuilt from the definitions:** the matrix M(y) of x ↦ p·x·q for y = (p, q) ∈ ℝ⁸ (own quaternion product; MᵗM = \|p\|²\|q\|²·I and det M = (\|p\|²\|q\|²)² checked), and the eight Leibniz sums Φ_k(M(y)), k ∈ {0,1,2,12,39,67,70,72}, as polynomials of degree 8 from the value vectors of the f_k.
- **Read as data** (two JSON files; a strict term parser; nothing executed): 2 × 8 cubic "features" F, four signed coordinate maps S₀,…,S₃, and an 8 × 8 rational matrix T with integer inverse. These are the witness of the identity, not objects the identity is about.
- **Identity.** Put u⁽ᵉ,ʲ⁾(y) = T⁻¹F⁽ᵉ⁾(S_j y) ∈ ℝ⁸ for the two parities e and the four maps j, and Γ_kl = Σ_{e,j} u_k u_l. Modulo N = \|p\|² − \|q\|² (normal form by p₀² ↦ \|q\|² − p₁² − p₂² − p₃²):
  - 2Φ_k = \|p\|²·Γ_kk for the seven minor generators;
  - 2Φ₇₀ = \|p\|²·(Γ₆₆ − 2Γ₀₂ − 2Γ₂₃) (positions 0, 2, 3, 6 are f₀, f₂, f₁₂, f₇₀): the (PB) matrix;
  - 2Φ₇₀ = \|p\|²·(Γ₆₆ + 2Γ₂₄ − 2Γ₂₅) (positions 4, 5 are f₃₉, f₆₇): the (CQ) matrix.
  
  All nine hold. A control with one sign flipped fails, so the check is not vacuous.
- **Consequence (PROOF-READ).** For unit p, q: 2Φ_g(O) = Σ_{e,j} uᵗG u with G the document's matrix, so Φ_g ≥ 0 when G is positive semidefinite; and 2Φ₇₀ ≥ −2⟨u₀,u₂⟩ − 2⟨u₂,u₃⟩ ≥ −4\|x₂\|(\|x₀\| + \|x₁₂\|), because \|u_k\|² = 2x_k². Every O ∈ SO(4) is x ↦ p·x·q for some unit p, q (classical double cover, CITED).

The maps S_j are (p, q) ↦ (e·p, q·ē) for e = 1, i, j, k (exact); this is not needed for the proof. The chain configuration of (PB) has an orbit of 576 under G, as PROOF.md states, and (CQ) is its image under g\*.

So the proof of the chain inequality rests on a certificate that comes from the supplement's data, and the certificate is correct. I did not derive the features independently and did not look for another proof.

The supplement document follows exactly that mechanism and proves the statement of Proposition 4.3 for ν = 34. So the two prove the same statement by the same route, with all details in the supplement only. The proof of Proposition 4.3 in the paper rescales the spectra by a fourth root of α; the document does not need this, since it works with x ∈ X directly.
