## Statement and scope

Case $T_{35}$: if $x\in X$ and $c(x)\in N_{T_{35}}$, then $\Phi_{c(x)}(O)\ge0$ for every $O\in\mathrm{SO}(4)$.

- **Non-strict contacts.** Only $c(x)\in N_T$ is used (gaps $\ge0$, $=0$ on $T$). Further contacts are allowed. PROOF-READ.
- **Every $O$.** Each branch of the proof ends with $c(x)$ written as a nonnegative combination of minor vectors plus one of the cited combinations, each of which has $\Phi\ge0$ on all of $\mathrm{SO}(4)$.
- **All spectra.** The proof never uses spectra. It uses only that $x\in X$, through $\nabla C(x)=0$, which holds on all of $X$ because it is a polynomial identity on the image of $\Psi$ (EXACT) and $X$ is its closure. Repeated eigenvalues and $a_i+b_j=0$ are therefore covered by the same argument; nothing is delegated. The representation of points of $X$ by finite spectra (PROOF.md 3.3) is not needed for this case.
- **No curve and no limit of spectra.** The only limits are limits of polynomial inequalities in the eight real coefficients (step 19).

### Boundaries (step 19)

The document proves its inequalities on open charts and extends them by continuity "in the original finite coefficients". I checked each extension.

- **Hard set.** $\{L_ee<1,\ L_jj<1\}$ is open in the closed orthant, and $L_e,L_j$ are continuous. Equality in (5) or (6) is outside it and is safe.
- **$ab=1$, $bq=1$.** Limits of chart points inside the hard set; (7), (9) and the two Bernstein inequalities are closed conditions. The strict positivity needed afterwards comes from the right side of (7), which is continuous and positive on the hard set.
- **$b=0$.** Positivity of $k_0$ and of $G\vert_{c=0}$ from the two explicit products; the Bernstein inequalities for $P_*=1+1/c$ by continuity from $b>0$.
- **$b=1$.** Limit $B\to\infty$ of the first family and the point $B=0$ of chart (15).
- **$c=0$.** Excluded by (9) for every $p$, so no threshold containing $1/c$ is needed there.
- **$e=0$, $j=0$.** $E=0$, $J=0$, $U=0$, $V=0$ are points of the charts.
- **$a=0$ or $q=0$ with $b\ge1$.** Not in chart (15), where $X,Y>0$. As $a\to0$ with $e$ fixed, $0\le\delta_1<ab$ forces $\delta_1\to0$, so $P_*$ tends to $r^2/c+r/b$ with $r=1-\delta_2$. At $a=0$ this is a valid threshold (one four-coefficient piece and the chain piece; $e f_1$ is an unused minor term), and the Bernstein inequalities hold there as limits. PROOF-READ. Exact evaluation at 443 rational points of these layers (test 3) found no violation.
- **$r=0$.** For $r\le0$ the point is safe by the two four-coefficient pieces; no table is needed.
- **$p=0$.** $k_0>0$.

## Observations

- **The hard branch is thin but not empty.** The points found have $0<d\le0.011$ when the gaps sum to 1 (the search was not designed to find the largest possible $d$), and all of them are safe already by one of the two four-coefficient conditions. I did not find a realizable point that needs the old-H region or the allocation chart; whether such points exist is not decided here and does not matter for the proof.
- **Coincident eigenvalues need no separate argument** in this case, unlike the group IV cases.
- The paper's Table 1 text for group V speaks of "the region where all Gram criteria fail"; in this document that region is $\{L_ee<1,L_jj<1\}$ cut by the two thresholds, and it is shown to contain no zero of $G$.
- The document's term counts are those of "numerator over the product of the reduced denominators, each to the degree of its variable"; with that convention all nineteen counts are reproduced.
