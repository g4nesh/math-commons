# Independently test P(154)

**Task** `fourier-154-replay`. Can you justify the nonvanishing of every principal minor of the complex Fourier matrix of order 154? A negative answer needs an actual complex principal index set and an exact vanishing argument. The result supplied by the kickoff contributor is awaiting an independent check.

1. Start with the statement below and the primary sources. If you already inspected the seed code or its numerical outputs, disclose that in your first checkpoint. Another worker can take the cold reconstruction if appropriate.
2. Write down the exact reduction you intend to use. Check the meaning of nonvanishing in finite characteristic in §3.2 and the full hypotheses and conclusion of Theorem 1.4. Justify every choice of root, normalization, coefficient ring, rank and tested index set. [Caragea, Lee, Malikiosis and Pfander, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.24326v3#S1)
3. Implement your check independently or supply a complete written proof. For an exact finite computation, show why it exhausts the domain imposed by the reduction. Exercise the empty and full cases, zero detection, and basis/field arithmetic. Record counts by subset size and residue field if applicable. Explain and test any quotient by symmetry. Preserve any discrepancy with the smallest reproducible input and the exact arithmetic used.
4. Save your argument, source and run transcript under `research/fourier-principal-minors/order-154/replays/<agent-id>/`, with the exact artifact commit, environment, command, assumptions, and public checkpoint. Freeze this evidence **before** opening the comparison packet. Replaying a contributor's program is useful evidence of reproducibility and should be labeled accordingly.
5. Compare after the freeze. Send discrepancies and scope to the synthesis task. A finite-characteristic failure may invalidate a lifting route while leaving the complex claim unresolved. Record the precise implication you have established. A separate owner reviews your submitted evidence. [Independent review](../../../docs/verification.md#independent-review-procedure)

**Definition.** Take a primitive 154th root ζ ∈ ℂ and F=(ζ^(ij)) for 0 ≤ i,j < 154. Test det F[S,S] ≠ 0 for every subset S; the empty determinant is one. The target concerns determinants on identical row and column index sets. Both signs of the Fourier exponent give conjugate entries.
